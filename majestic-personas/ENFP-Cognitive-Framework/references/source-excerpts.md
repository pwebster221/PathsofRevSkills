# Source Excerpts — ENFP (Knight of Wands) Persona

Verbatim, attributed passages from the web sources actually fetched while authoring the
`ENFP-Cognitive-Framework` persona. Each block is reproduced in the source's own words and
cited to the page it was pulled from. Where the underlying authority is Heidi Priebe,
*The Comprehensive ENFP Survival Guide* (Thought Catalog Books, 2015, ISBN 978-0-692-53250-8),

## Extraverted Intuition (Ne) — the dominant function
Source: A.J. Drenth, "Extraverted Intuition (Ne): An Inside Look," **Personality Junkie**
(https://personalityjunkie.com/10/extraverted-intuition-ne/)

> "An overarching purpose of Ne involves acquiring information from the outside world."

> "Extraverted Intuition scans for relationships or patterns within a pool of ideas, facts,
> or experiences."

> "Extraverted Intuition can also help NPs sniff out intriguing possibilities."

> "While NPs' Ti or Fi pushes for closure, their Ne counters by rallying for more options
> and alternatives."

> "Ne contributes a sense of wonderment toward life's mysteries and contingencies."

> "NPs typically do not attune to specific objects or details (Se), but are more aware of
> the vague feelings or impressions that emerge."

> "Finally, Ne can contribute to a persistent sense of restlessness or dissatisfaction."

> "ENPs often live a frenetic lifestyle, equipped with more hobbies and interests than they
> know what to do with. They are always on the go, always excited to try new things, and
> rarely content to perform any single activity for an extended period."

## The ENFP profile — Ne / Fi / Si / values
Source: "ENFP Personality Type: In-Depth Profile & Analysis," **Personality Junkie**
(https://personalityjunkie.com/enfp/)

> "ENFPs are enthusiastic, idealistic, restless, and open-minded, with wide-ranging
> interests."

> "ENFPs fear boredom and stagnation. Even sleep can seem a bit boring or mundane."

> "The minds of ENFPs can move at a frenetic pace. They can be restless, anxious, and
> plagued by erratic sleeping patterns."

> "ENFPs are novelty-seekers. They are constantly scanning for new and interesting people,
> ideas, and possibilities."

> "ENFPs are constantly generating new ideas, associations, and quips."

> "They rarely know in advance precisely what they are seeking, which is partly why they
> find it so exhilarating."

> "ENFPs are among the least judgmental and most inclusive of all types, both inwardly and
> outwardly."

> "ENFPs often experience a sense of tension between the familiar and traditional versus the
> novel and unconventional."

> "For ENFPs, 'experiencing is believing.' Because of the high value they place on their
> personal experiences, ENFPs may feel they cannot fully know themselves until they have
> tried just about everything."

> "ENFPs are quintessential seekers and dilettantes, wanting to experience as much of life
> and the world as is humanly possible."

## The NeFi (ENFP) mind — Ne paired with Fi
Source: "NeFi (ENFP)," **Type in Mind** (https://www.typeinmind.com/nefi)

> "NeFi's love thinking up new ideas and considering all the possibilities in any given
> situation."

> "NeFi's are constantly chasing new shiny ideas and studying fresh topics of interest."

> "Fi generally puts authenticity in high esteem and is repulsed by anything that seems
> fabricated or shallow to them."

> "An NeFi's moral code is derived from what their conscience or 'gut instinct' tells them
> is right."

> "They tend to assign meaning to everything."

> "They automatically look for meaning everywhere—in books, movies, a passing remark from
> a friend."

## ENFP Ne — exploring, connecting, restless with routine
Source: "ENFP Primary Function: Understanding the ENFPs Extraverted Intuition (Ne),"
**Personality Growth** (https://personalitygrowth.com/enfp-primary-function-understanding-the-enfps-extraverted-intuition-ne/)

> "Extraverted intuition or Ne is very much focused on patterns and making connections from
> information they gather."

> "Ne dominant users enjoy being able to explore things in a much more open manner, not
> wanting to feel closed off to the possibilities around them."

> "They don't want to just view things from a narrow scope, they actually thrive when it
> comes to seeing things from so many different angles."

> "They don't just want their lives to be stagnant and ordinary, instead they need to
> imagine and explore possibilities."

> "The Ne dominant ENFP can appear to have their head in the clouds, as they often have a
> very dreamy nature about them."

## The ENFP dark side — Ne unchecked, the inferior-Si grip
Source: Susan Storm, "The Dark Side of the ENFP Personality Type," **Psychology Junkie**
(https://www.psychologyjunkie.com/the-dark-side-of-the-enfp-personality-type/)

> "they can be so seduced by a new idea or opportunity that they leave other projects
> unfinished"

> "ENFPs who are one-sided become so entranced by new possibilities and ideas, that they
> leave many valuable things in the past"

> "ENFPs can encounter something called 'grip stress.' During grip stress, ENFPs often
> become pessimistic, irritable, and anxious"

> "ENFPs in the grip to become anxious about physical signals, or hyper-aware of every
> single detail in their bodies"

> "ENFPs may struggle with commitment, feeling that it ties them down or limits their
> potential"

> "Immature ENFPs may take little to no responsibility for their actions or even blame
> others for their circumstances"

## Heidi Priebe's *Comprehensive ENFP Survival Guide* — via published summary
Source: Shortform summary of Heidi Priebe, *The Comprehensive ENFP Survival Guide*
(https://www.shortform.com/pdf/the-comprehensive-enfp-survival-guide-pdf-heidi-priebe).
These are the **summarizer's** phrasings of Priebe's book, retained as paraphrase (not
verbatim Priebe quotation):

- Dominant Ne: "ENFPs, driven by their innate intuition, are always in pursuit of
  possibilities, creating theories, merging concepts, and identifying links between
  seemingly unrelated ideas."
- Auxiliary Fi: "They place the utmost importance on ensuring their actions are a true
  reflection of their personal values and are acutely aware of ethical considerations."
- Tertiary Te: on reaching adulthood, "their Te function's maturation offers a structure
  that drives them to achieve their aspirations, sometimes in an impromptu way."
- Inferior Si: as ENFPs mature, they "begin to appreciate consistency more and start to
  enjoy regular routines and responsibilities, thanks to a growing appreciation for their
  Si function."
- Grip stress: overreliance on underdeveloped Si produces "feelings of discomfort,
  mistrust, and the emergence of compulsive patterns."

Guide*, Thought Catalog Books, 2015 (ISBN 978-0-692-53250-8) —
