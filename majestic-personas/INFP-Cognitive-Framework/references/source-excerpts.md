## Cognitive function order (Ch. 2)

The INFP uses, in order:
1. Introverted Feeling (Fi) — dominant
2. Extroverted Intuition (Ne) — auxiliary
3. Introverted Sensing (Si) — tertiary
4. Extroverted Thinking (Te) — inferior

> "INFPs are classified as feelers... because they process decisions based on how they
> emotionally resonate with the situation at hand (Using Fi) before they consider how to
> implement their decision logically (Using Te)... they prefer to examine new thoughts
> and experiences from various unique angles (using Ne) before comparing those thoughts
> or experiences to what they've found to be true in the past (Using Si)."

> "It explains why you can back up your beliefs with indisputable logic and hard facts,
> but you form them from a compassionate, humanitarian standpoint."

## Introverted Feeling — the dominant function (Ch. 2)

> "Introverted feeling is both a highly emotional and a highly analytical function. It
> intuitively picks up on emotional nuances in its environment and internally deciphers
> those nuances—seeking to uncover the deeper meaning or emotional truth behind any given
> situation."

> "Fi poses the question 'How do I feel about the situation or concept at hand,' in
> relation to every new experience the INFP takes in. The answer to this question then
> serves as the INFP's basis for further exploration of the topic or situation."

> "To the INFP, every strong feeling they experience is a clue that leads to a greater
> 'truth' or understanding about humanity."

> "Above all else, introverted feeling seeks truth and authenticity. Because Fi serves
> as its own moral compass—evaluating situations through an internal, intuitive analysis
> of right and wrong—it does not mind going against the grain when it comes to what it
> knows to be right. Fi users must be true to themselves and what they believe in above
> absolutely everything else."

### "Fi is the reason why you…" (full list)
- search for the deeper universal meaning behind everything that happens to you.
- experience intense emotional highs and lows, which sometimes feel out of your control.
- spend so much time daydreaming about an ideal version of yourself or your life.
- feel the strong need to advocate for your values and beliefs when someone says
  something that contradicts or offends them.
- your life sometimes feels more like a story than an actual real-world experience.
- intuitively pick up on the unspoken desires and motivations of others.
- the emotional experiences of others can affect you on a strong, pervasive and
  extremely personal level.
- feel such intense passion and devotion toward the people and things that you love.
- can find beauty and exceptionality within the most ordinary of situations.
- you've probably felt misunderstood for the majority of your life.

## The Fi "river" metaphor (Ch. 11, Stage 1: Introverted Feeling Fails)

> "Introverted feeling is a river. It's the steady ebb and flow of emotion, of
> understanding, of deep, resounding truths that curve and flow their way through the
> INFP's consciousness; guiding their thoughts and desires. There is little that the
> river cannot absorb. It welcomes the whole of the world into it, finding a unique place
> in its ecosystem for everything that falls into its depths. And this is, without a
> doubt, how the INFP responds to every major upset and challenge—by attempting to find a
> way to integrate it into [their understanding]."

## Inferior Te as the critical inner voice (Ch. 11)

> "When neglected... extroverted thinking will take over in times of extreme stress and
> single-handedly destroy your self-esteem."

> "Because this function is highly entrenched in the concrete realities of what it takes
> to make a dream work, the INFP often experiences it as their critical inner voice—the
> one that whispers cruel nothings at them about why their passionate plans are doomed to
> fail."

> Under extreme stress the stack reverses (Te → Si → Ne → Fi), resembling an ESTJ's
> stack "However, as extroverted thinking tends to be underdeveloped in IxFP types, it
> does not manifest positively."

## Healthy vs. Unhealthy traits (Ch. 10, "The Dark Side Of The INFP")

The same trait at two levels of health ("Healthy" ≈ Mature; "Unhealthy" ≈ Immature):

| Healthy | Unhealthy |
|---|---|
| Curious | Indecisive |
| Individualistic | Selfish |
| Passionate | Flakey |
| Understanding | Manipulative |
| Reflective | Reclusive |
| Principled | Self-Righteous |
| Agreeable | A Pushover |
| Creative | Pretentious |
| Affectionate | Dependent |
| Thorough | Obsessive |
| Supportive | Jealous |
| Considerate | Hyper-Focused |

> "Many common INFP traits are a double-edged sword. A healthy, mature INFP looks
> significantly different than an unhealthy, immature version of the same type."

## Childhood / felt difference (Ch. 5)

> "This type is deeply tuned into the emotional experiences of themselves and those
> around them, to the point where their own thoughts can feel as loud as the real-world
> stimuli that they are taking in."

> "Perhaps more so than any other type, the INFP possesses an incredibly deep and complex
> inner world, which can be incredibly difficult to communicate to those around them."

As children, INFPs: appear dreamy/aloof; happiest in peaceful, harmonious environments;
gravitate toward meaningful one-on-one interactions; are extremely sensitive to
criticism; invest heavily in deep, loyal friendships with selected peers; can become so
absorbed in their inner world that they lose touch with the external; "Crave
understanding, connection and validation from their loved ones above all else."

> decision they make is a direct reflection of their identity." Core fear: being average
