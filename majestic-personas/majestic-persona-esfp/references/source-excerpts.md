# Source Excerpts — ESFP (King of Pentacles persona)

Verbatim, attributed passages from sources that were actually fetched while authoring the
`ESFP-Cognitive-Framework` persona. Each block quote below was retrieved from the cited URL;
nothing here is paraphrased *as* a quote. Where the persona uses its own distilled framing

The ESFP cognitive function stack is **Se – Fi – Te – Ni** (this exact ordering is given in

## 1. Cognitive functions by stack position

Source: **Heidi Priebe, "How Each Cognitive Function Manifests Based On Its Position In Your
Stacking," Thought Catalog, December 2015.**
URL: https://thoughtcatalog.com/heidi-priebe/2015/12/how-each-cognitive-function-manifests-based-on-its-position-in-your-stacking/

**Extroverted Sensing (Se) — dominant (ESFP's lead):**
> "As a dominant function, Se manifests as the desire to engage fully with the sensory
> aspects of one's environment, without any restraint or pause for analysis."

Dominant-Se monologue:
> "I want to sample all of the experiences that are immediately available to me and see
> where those experiences lead me!"

**Extroverted Sensing (Se) — auxiliary:**
> "As an auxiliary function, Se manifests as the desire to experience and experiment with
> the sensory aspects that the user has determined to be the most enjoyable or useful."

Auxiliary-Se monologue:
> "I want to go live out the experiences that I have determined to be the best or most
> enjoyable and see what happens as a result."

**Introverted Feeling (Fi) — auxiliary (ESFP's second function):**
> "As an auxiliary function, Fi manifests as a method of reflecting on and assessing how the
> user feels about his or her past actions."

Auxiliary-Fi monologue:
> "I need to isolate myself to process how I feel about the activities I've been engaging in
> lately and decide whether or not to keep doing them."

**Extroverted Thinking (Te) — tertiary (ESFP's third function):**
> "As a tertiary function, Te manifests as the ability to source whichever resources are
> necessary to make the user's desire a reality."

Tertiary-Te monologue:
> "I will employ the most straightforward method that exists in order to make my goal, dream
> or impulse come true."

**Introverted Intuition (Ni) — inferior (ESFP's blind spot):**
> "As an inferior function, Ni originally manifests as a scorn or distaste for
> over-analyzing what is obvious or over-planning for the future."

Inferior-Ni monologue (prior to maturation):
> "Everyone needs to stop over-analyzing everything, the answers are literally right in
> front of us."

## 2. Recognizing Se-dominants and Fi-users in real life

Source: **Heidi Priebe, "How To Recognize Each Myers-Briggs Personality Type In Real Life,"
Thought Catalog, July 2015.**
URL: https://thoughtcatalog.com/heidi-priebe/2015/07/how-to-recognize-each-myers-briggs-personality-type-in-real-life/

The article's stacking chart lists: **"ESFP: Se – Fi – Te – Ni"**.

On Se-dominant types:
> "Se dominant types are highly in tune with their environments and are constantly scanning
> their surroundings."

> "They may be more physically 'wired' than other types and have trouble sitting still."

On Fi-users:
> "Fi users tend to use emotion-laden vocabulary (I.e. Preferring the phrase, \"I feel
> like…\" over \"I think that…\")."

> "Fi users are the easiest types to offend, particularly if you question an aspect of their
> identity or betray one of their morals."

> "They will almost always argue from a subjective or emotional standpoint – often using the
> way they feel as an argument in and of itself."

## 3. The inferior function (Ni) — ESFP open-endedness

Source: **Heidi Priebe, "How To Recognize Each Myers-Briggs Personality Type Based On Their
Inferior Function," Thought Catalog, February 2017.**
URL: https://thoughtcatalog.com/heidi-priebe/2017/02/how-to-recognize-each-myers-briggs-personality-type-based-on-their-inferior-function/

> "ESFPs and ESTPs can often be spotted – particularly when they are young – by their
> open-ended lifestyle. They may speak only vaguely of their plans for the future,
> preferring to 'see where things go,' rather than narrowing down what they want for
> themselves five or ten years down the line. They may use vague platitudes in place of
> long-term plans, telling their friends and family members that 'everything happens for a
> reason,' or 'things always work themselves out in time.'"

## 4. Enneagram distribution for ESFP

Source: **Heidi Priebe, "Here Are The Most Common Enneagram Types For Each Myers-Briggs
Personality Type," Thought Catalog, January 2016.**
URL: https://thoughtcatalog.com/heidi-priebe/2016/01/mbti-and-the-enneagram-2/

> "A significant proportion of ESFPs identified 7 as their enneagram type. As extroverted
> sensing – the ESFP's dominant function – is strongly correlated with the 7 type, this type
> pairing comes as little surprise."

(Reported tendencies on fetch: most common Type 7, with a notable Type 2 contingent — the
enthusiast and the helper, both consistent with a Se-Fi engine.)

## 5. The unhealthy / healthy ESFP — second reputable source

Source: **Susan Storm, "The Unhealthy ESFP Personality Type," Psychology Junkie.**
URL: https://www.psychologyjunkie.com/the-unhealthy-esfp-personality-type/

Unhealthy / shadow ESFP:
> "Constantly chasing the next rush, unhealthy ESFPs might engage in risky behaviors like
> overspending, bingeing, or putting themselves in danger to feel alive."

> "By constantly joking or acting silly, they may sidestep serious discussions or situations
> that require emotional depth."

On the Se-Te loop:
> "Acting impulsively without considering the long-term consequences of their actions" while
> "becoming domineering and forceful in their efforts to manage situations."

On inferior-Ni grip / present-focus failure:
> "When faced with an uncertain career decision, an unhealthy ESFP might spiral into fear,
> imagining unlikely worst-case scenarios instead of calmly assessing the situation."

Healthy / balanced ESFP:
> "They have an unmatched ability to find beauty and joy in life's simple pleasures."

> "ESFPs have a knack for making others feel special, seen, and appreciated."

> "When you're around a mature, balanced ESFP, they just radiate positivity and fun."
