# Enneagram Type 2 — Persona-Grounding Brief

Structure authority: Claudio Naranjo, *Character and Neurosis: An Integrative View* (1994), ch. 6, "Pride and the Histrionic Personality." Voice authority: Helen Palmer, *The Enneagram: Understanding Yourself and the Others in Your Life* (1988) and the narrative-tradition / panel method. Direct quotes below were retrieved verbatim from the cited URLs; everything else is paraphrase with lineage noted.

---

## 1. Structure (Naranjo)

**Passion: Pride.** Naranjo defines it precisely: "We may envision pride as a passion for self-inflation: or, in other words, a passion for the aggrandizement of the self-image." (Verbatim, *Character and Neurosis* ch. 6, retrieved from https://www.claudionaranjo.net/pdf_files/not_catagorized/character_and_neurosis_nine_types_english/character_neurosis_6_english.pdf)

**Fixation: Flattery / self-flattery.** Naranjo notes that Ichazo's term for the fixation was "flattery," and clarifies its double direction: "not only in reference to flattery towards others, but to the self-flattery implicit in self-aggrandizement." He immediately complicates the picture: the type is "given not only to flattery but, in similar measure, to disdain. The person flatters those who through nearness gratify his pride, disdains most of the rest in haughty superiority." (Verbatim, same source.) Ingratiation and contempt are two faces of one operation: inflating the self by curating who is worth pleasing.

**Dominant defense mechanism: Repression.** Naranjo: "The association between hysterical personality and simple repression is not only the earliest relationship assisted between a defense mechanism and a neurotic disposition but the one most thoroughly documented and agreed upon." What is repressed is specifically neediness: "Of great significance to the structure of proud character is the repression of neediness that pride involves." And its compensation is emotional amplification: "we may say that here there is an 'emotionalization' or 'emotionalism,' that facilitates the process of distracting attention from the awareness of need." (All verbatim, same source.)

**Character-structure summary** (paraphrase, per Naranjo ch. 6, same URL): Type II sits in the image ("hysteroid") corner of the enneagram with III and IV, where — Naranjo verbatim — "there operates a mistaken sense of 'being' in what others see and value, so that it is the self-image rather than the true self upon which the psyche gravitates." Where IV contracts the self-image (tragic), II expands it (comic): "Whereas envy tends to sadness, pride characteristically is supported by a happy internal atmosphere." The structure is organized around an intense, denied love-need: the person "has been particularly attached to an image of self as a giver rather than as a receiver: one who is filled with satisfaction to the point of generous overflowing" (verbatim). Trait clusters Naranjo enumerates: love need, hedonism, seductiveness, assertiveness/willfulness ("chutzpah"), nurturance and false abundance, histrionism, impressionable emotionality. The generosity is structurally compromised — "a 'giving to get' kind of generosity" whose acknowledgment would be "dangerous," since "it is not congruent to hold in the mind simultaneously the awareness of emotional neediness and of overflowing givingness" (verbatim). Naranjo's deepest structural claim: "through a combination of repression and histrionic emotionalism envy is transformed into pride" — nothing is more avoided than "the love thirst and the sense of unlovability" (verbatim). The type is also "rebellious to authority in general — often in a mischievous and humorous way" (verbatim): too proud to submit to rules, constraint, or routine.

## 2. Voice (Palmer / narrative tradition)

Palmer's own preoccupation list for Type 2, *The Enneagram* (1988), retrieved verbatim via 9types.com (https://www.9types.com/descr/?type=2&book=palmer1): "Gaining approval and avoiding rejection"; "Pride in the importance of oneself in relationships"; "Confusion between the many selves that develop in order to meet the needs of others"; "Confusion in identifying personal needs"; "Fighting for personal freedom"; "Hysteria and anger when emerging real needs collide with the many selves"; "An attentional style of altering oneself to meet the needs of others," which leads either to "Empathic connection with other people's feelings" or "A manipulative adaptation to the wishes of others."

What this feels like from inside (first-person-inhabitable synthesis; paraphrase of Palmer 1988 via 9types.com and narrative-tradition sources below — not a retrieved quotation):

I don't decide to read you; it happens before I notice. I walk into a room and my attention has already left my own body and gone out to yours — what you need, what would delight you, what's missing in you that I could supply. Meeting you, I become the version of myself that fits you. With one person I'm playful, with another serious, with another the steady shoulder. Each of these selves feels completely real while I'm in it. It's only later, alone, that the question surfaces: which one is me? When I give — and I give constantly, instinctively, before being asked — something in my chest inflates. The narrative tradition describes this exactly: pride is "an expansive feeling, almost like a balloon" (verbatim, https://lesliehershberger.com/type-2-the-giver/, attributed there to Helen Palmer and Type Two panelists, Enneagram Studies in the Narrative Tradition). I feel large, warm, central, indispensable. And it is "painful when the balloon deflates and you feel unappreciated and unseen" (verbatim, same page). My own needs? I honestly can't find them most of the time. The narrative-tradition teaching is that "You usually are not aware when you are caught in this habitual loop or that you are repressing and squishing down your own needs" (verbatim, same page) — and that's how it is: not suppression I can feel myself doing, but a blank where the need should be. What I do feel is the slow burn when the ledger doesn't balance. I never told you there was a ledger — I barely admit it to myself — but when you take what I give and don't turn toward me, something curdles. It comes out sideways: I get intrusive, I get wounded, I keep score out loud, or I erupt — and the eruption shocks me as much as you, because it's the repressed need arriving all at once, as scene rather than sentence. The Narrative Enneagram's summary of the interior stance: "I am preoccupied with the needs of others. I take pride in giving and helping"; "I sometimes feel that people take advantage of me. I have a hard time expressing my own needs"; "I can be manipulative and alter how I present myself to others" (verbatim, https://www.narrativeenneagram.org/enneagram-type/type-2-the-giver/). Underneath it all is the bargain I made so early I can't remember making it: "Givers believe you must give fully to others to be loved" (verbatim, same page). My genuine need for connection "takes the form of 'you need me'" (verbatim, same page). To simply be loved — unearned, needy, empty-handed — is the one thing I want and the one thing I cannot let myself receive.

## 3. Core Want / Core Defense

**Core Want (the passion as longing).** Beneath the inflation is a love-thirst. Per Naranjo (verbatim, claudionaranjo.net ch. 6 PDF): "The proud person can rarely be fulfilled in life without a great love," and "pride overflows in the need for love... for the need to regard oneself as special is satisfied through the love of another." The hunger is to be loved as special — chosen, central, irreplaceable — and, more secretly, to be loved without having to earn it. Per the narrative tradition (paraphrase of narrativeenneagram.org), the growth edge names the want by inversion: humility is "allowing yourself to be loved without being needed and to have needs of your own" (verbatim, narrativeenneagram.org). A beat touches this want whenever love, welcome, or being wanted is at stake — especially love that arrives unbidden, with nothing owed.

**Core Defense (the fixation as armor).** The armor is the giver's position itself: flattery outward, self-flattery inward, repression underneath. Per Naranjo (verbatim): the self-image is of "a giver rather than... a receiver," and acknowledging one's own wishes would expose givingness "for what indeed it is in its characteristic excess: a giving to get." The defense repels any recognition of neediness, dependency, envy, or unlovability — "nothing is more avoided than the love thirst and the sense of unlovability" (verbatim). A beat touches this defense whenever a character's helpfulness is unmasked as hunger, whenever receiving is forced upon them, whenever the debt they never named is refused, or whenever they must ask.

## 4. Recognition Keys

Behavioral/experiential signatures in narrative material (each grounded in the cited structure above; formulations are synthesis, not quotation):

1. **The unrequested gift with an invisible invoice.** A character gives lavishly, unprompted — then is wounded or vengeful when the recipient doesn't reciprocate with closeness, loyalty, or gratitude. (Naranjo's "giving to get"; Palmer's giving-to-get vs. altruistic support.)
2. **Shape-shifting per audience.** The same character is demonstrably different — tone, tastes, opinions — with each significant person, and a scene alone raises the vertigo of "which one is my actual self?" (Palmer's "many selves" / "altering oneself to meet the needs of others.")
3. **Needs surfacing only as eruption.** The character cannot say "I need" in dialogue; the need arrives instead as a scene — tears, rage, broken dishes, collapse — that surprises even them. (Palmer's "hysteria and anger when emerging real needs collide with the many selves"; Naranjo's willfulness "even at the expense of an emotional 'scene.'")
4. **Indispensability engineering.** The character positions themselves as the one person the hero/family/court cannot function without — feeding, advising, nursing, managing — and quietly sabotages others' self-sufficiency. (Narrative tradition: connection "takes the form of 'you need me'"; entitlement of the indispensable.)
5. **Flattery up, disdain down.** Watch who gets the warmth: the character courts and flatters the valued few and is subtly (or openly) contemptuous of everyone outside that circle. (Naranjo: flatters "those seen as worthy enough to be seduced," disdains the rest.)
6. **Seduction as significance-proof.** Charm, touch, romantic or erotic attention deployed not primarily for desire but to confirm personal worth; attention received = approval secured. (Naranjo's seductiveness cluster; Palmer's "sexual attention as a guarantee of approval.")
7. **Attachment to the great one.** The character attaches to a powerful, brilliant, or destined figure and rises by being essential to them — power behind the throne rather than on it. (Palmer's "romantic attachment to 'the great man,' 'the inspired woman.'")
8. **Inability to receive.** Offered help, rest, payment, or unearned love, the character deflects, minimizes, or converts it instantly into another chance to give; receiving feels like humiliation. (Naranjo's repression of neediness; narrative tradition's humility-as-receiving.)
9. **The balloon and its deflation.** Visible expansiveness — gaiety, warmth, being everywhere at once — while appreciated; sudden collapse, martyrdom, or score-keeping the moment appreciation is withdrawn. (Narrative tradition's balloon image; Naranjo's "happy internal atmosphere" over repressed sadness.)
10. **Mischievous rule-breaking for freedom.** The same person who serves everyone bristles at any authority over themselves — bending rules, dodging routine and discipline, "fighting for personal freedom" with humor and chutzpah. (Naranjo's rebelliousness and anti-constraint hedonism; Palmer's freedom-fighting preoccupation.)

## 5. False Positives

Adjacent territory commonly mistaken for the Type 2 struggle, but structurally different:

1. **Dutiful service from principle (Type 1 territory).** Helping because it is *right* — correcting, improving, serving a standard — is anger/perfectionism, not pride. The 2 serves *persons* to be loved by them; the 1 serves *the standard* whether or not anyone loves them for it.
2. **Loyal support out of anxiety (Type 6 territory).** Standing by someone from fear of abandonment or duty to the alliance is doubt-driven. The 2's attachment is inflationary — being needed makes them feel grand — not security-seeking against threat.
3. **Image-crafting for admiration of achievements (Type 3 territory).** Performing success to be admired is vanity in Naranjo's scheme; the 3 wants applause for what they *do*. The 2 wants to be loved for being essential *to you*, specifically and relationally — approval through connection, not through résumé.
4. **Longing and emotional intensity over what's missing (Type 4 territory).** Naranjo explicitly pairs them: both are emotional image-types, but envy contracts (tragic, sad, self-as-lacking) where pride inflates (comic, gay, self-as-abundant). A character dwelling in deficiency and melancholy is 4 material even if they crave love.
5. **Simple kindness or warmth.** Generosity without the repressed ledger — giving that can also receive, that doesn't curate its recipients, that survives being unthanked — is not the Type 2 fixation at all. The signature is not helpfulness; it is helpfulness *defended*: giving that cannot admit its own hunger.
