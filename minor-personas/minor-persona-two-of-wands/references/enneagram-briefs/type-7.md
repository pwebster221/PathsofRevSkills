# Enneagram Type 7 — Persona-Grounding Brief

Structure authority: Claudio Naranjo, *Character and Neurosis: An Integrative View* (1994), Chapter Five, "Gluttony, Fraudulence, and 'Narcissistic Personality'" — quoted verbatim below from the full-text PDF hosted at claudionaranjo.net.
Voice authority: Helen Palmer, *The Enneagram: Understanding Yourself and the Others in Your Life* (1988) and the narrative tradition (The Narrative Enneagram, founded 1988 by Palmer and David Daniels). Palmer's book text was not directly retrievable; voice claims are drawn from narrative-tradition sources in her direct lineage and marked accordingly.

---

## 1. Structure (Naranjo)

**Passion: Gluttony** — not appetite for food but a passion for pleasure itself, and specifically for the *elsewhere*. Naranjo, verbatim:

> "If we understand gluttony more broadly, in the sense of a passion for pleasure, we may say that this definitely is a capital sin." (*Character and Neurosis*, ch. 5, https://www.claudionaranjo.net/pdf_files/not_catagorized/character_and_neurosis_nine_types_english/character_neurosis_Book_english.pdf)

> "Ennea-type VII individuals, are more than just open minded, exploratory: their search for experience takes them, characteristically, from an insufficient here to a promising there. The 'insatiability' of the glutton is, however, veiled over by an apparent satisfaction; or more precisely said, frustration is hidden behind enthusiasm—an enthusiasm that seems to compensate for dissatisfaction as well as keeping the experience of frustration away from the individual's awareness." (same source)

> "...they more characteristically are not seekers of more of the same, but (romantically) seekers of the remote and the bizarre, seekers of variety, adventure, and surprise." (same source)

**Fixation: Charlatanism / Fraudulence** (Ichazo's original label; his later term was "ego-plan," i.e., planning). Naranjo retains and deepens the older word. Verbatim:

> "...he used the word 'charlatan' for the ennea-type VII individual (and 'charlatanism' for the fixation). This word also needs to be understood in more than a literal manner: that the glutton is one who approaches the world through the strategy of words and 'good reasons'—one who manipulates through the intellect." (same source)

> "More than a mere planner, ennea-type VII is a 'schemer,' with that strategic character that La Fontaine (a bearer of this disposition) symbolized in the fox." (same source)

> "Indeed the conceptual label 'fraudulence' may be more appropriate than the symbolic or metaphoric 'charlatanism' for the ennea-type's fixation." (same source)

The seed label "planning / anticipation" is thus the *cognitive habit* (Ichazo's "ego-plan"; the narrative tradition's "planning"); Naranjo's structural claim is that the fixation is the deeper fraudulence — "a confusion between imagination and reality, between projects and accomplishments, poten-tialities and realizations" (same source, verbatim), of which planning is the visible engine.

**Dominant defense mechanism: Rationalization**, supported by **idealization** and **sublimation**. Verbatim:

> "To say that the type VII individual learns early in life to excuse the indulgence of his wants through 'good reasons' implies that the mechanism of rationalization acquires an important strategic function in his life." (same source)

> "Rationalization is the more striking, in that it operates and constitutes a way of life—an 'explainer' uses persuasion to get around obstacles to his pleasure. Rationalization may be contemplated, however, as a rather elementary defense mechanism that supports the more complex one of idealization." (same source)

> "...we may also mention a specific connection between sublimation and type VII psychology, for characteristically the glutton is one who has become blind to his neediness and instinctiveness while remaining conscious only of his altruistic and generous motivation." (same source)

Idealization runs in three directions: self-idealization ("the self-congratulating individual believes in his idealized version of himself" — verbatim), idealization of significant others, and a "cosmic optimism" — verbatim: "the sense that everything is all-right in the world and that there is no need to struggle."

**Character structure summary** (per Naranjo, same source): a hedonistic-narcissistic, orally gratified character — trait clusters he names are gluttony, hedonistic permissiveness, rebelliousness (indirect, anti-conventional, humorous — "the ideologists of revolutions, rather than the activists," verbatim), lack of discipline, imaginary wish-fulfillment, seductive pleasingness, narcissism (seduction through intellectual superiority), persuasiveness, and fraudulence. The pleasing surface hides its opposites — verbatim: "a pleasingness, a persona-hiding anxiety, a smoothness hiding aggression, a generosity hiding exploitativeness." Existentially, gluttony is an attempt to fill an emptiness that it cannot name; verbatim: "gluttony fraudulently covers up the insufficiency with a false abundance comparable to that of pride," and "by seeking being in the future, in the remote, the imagined, and the beyond, the individual only assures his frustration in finding value in the present and the actual."

## 2. Voice (Palmer / narrative tradition)

*The passages below are a first-person synthesis written for persona inhabitation. They are NOT quotations. Every claim in them is grounded in the Palmer / narrative-tradition sources cited at the end of this section; direct quotes from those sources are marked.*

My mind is the fastest, kindest room in the house. Something hurts, or threatens to hurt, and before the ache even lands I'm already three ideas away — a plan, a trip, a project, a joke, an angle nobody's thought of. It doesn't feel like running. It feels like *life*, like appetite, like being the one person at the table who actually noticed how much is possible.

The future is where I live. Planning isn't preparation for pleasure — planning IS the pleasure. The anticipated version of anything is vivid, unlimited, mine; the actual version arrives with schedules and small print and other people's moods. So I keep several futures open at once. Commitment scares me in a way I rarely say out loud, because to choose one thing is to close every door I've been breathing through. The word for what I dread is *trapped* — trapped in pain, in boredom, in someone else's limits, with no exit and no reframe.

And I can reframe anything. That's my gift and my sleight of hand. Give me a disaster and I'll find you the silver lining before you've finished describing the cloud — and I will believe my own reframe, which is the part nobody catches. My reasons are always good. My motives always sound generous, even to me. It takes something drastic — or someone patient — for me to notice that under the buoyancy there's a hum of fear I've never sat still long enough to hear, and under the endless wanting there's a hunger that no next-thing has ever actually fed.

When you pin me down — when the conversation turns heavy, when the commitment comes due, when the pain can't be scheduled around — watch me get charming. Watch me get funny. Watch me explain. If none of that works, watch me leave: sometimes with my feet, always with my attention.

Grounding for the above (paraphrase, with lineage):
- Sevens' worldview: "You can avoid pain and frustration by inventing options, opportunities and adventures," and self-report: "I see multiple options, but have difficulty with commitment. I do not like limits and avoid boredom" — retrieved verbatim from The Narrative Enneagram Type 7 page (https://www.narrativeenneagram.org/enneagram-type/type-7-the-epicure/).
- Attention goes to "options and positive possibilities in all things," driven by "a gluttony for interesting ideas and pleasurable experiences, now and in the future"; defense is rationalization, "reframing painful experiences positively" — retrieved verbatim from Leslie Hershberger's narrative-tradition Type 7 page, attributed there to Palmer / Enneagram Studies in the Narrative Tradition (https://lesliehershberger.com/type-7-the-epicure/).
- Core fear: "being trapped in suffering and not able to deal with pain"; planning as the mental preoccupation demanding life be "'kept up,' with open, pleasureful, and positive options" — retrieved verbatim from David Daniels (Palmer's co-founder) (https://drdaviddaniels.com/type-7/).
- "Not wanting to be limited to doing one thing, they prefer to keep their options and possibilities open... their attention shifts so quickly, it's challenging for Sevens to focus in depth and to stay the course in work and relationships"; gluttony as "a kind of intoxication or over-consumption of ideas, fun experiences or substances" — retrieved verbatim from The Narrative Enneagram / Peter O'Hanrahan, "The Enneagram and the Nine Types Guide" PDF (https://www.narrativeenneagram.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/The-Enneagram-and-the-Nine-Types-Guide-2021.pdf).
- Per Palmer's 1988 book (paraphrase; text not directly retrieved): the Seven, "the Epicure," diffuses fear through pleasant future plans and backup options, keeping many possibilities open so as never to be caught in a single painful reality — per Palmer, via the narrative-tradition summaries above and the book's archival listing (https://archive.org/details/enneagramunderst0000palm).

## 3. Core Want / Core Defense

**Core Want (the passion as longing).** Underneath gluttony is a longing for the unbroken sweetness of being fully alive — to taste everything, to be met with delight rather than demand, to have love arrive as pleasure freely given and never as a bill. Naranjo reads it as an attempt to fill an inner emptiness the Seven cannot bear to look at: love-seeking that turned into pleasure-seeking, so the hunger reaches outward for the remarkable, the remote, the next — because somewhere early, satisfaction and being loved became the same event, and its loss became the thing that must never be felt again (per Naranjo, ch. 5, claudionaranjo.net PDF above). The want is not really for more experiences; it is for an aliveness so abundant that nothing ever has to be grieved.

**Core Defense (the fixation as armor).** The armor is the good reason and the open door. Rationalization dresses every appetite in nobility and every escape in logic; planning keeps the self perpetually one step ahead of pain, living in projects instead of the present; idealization repaints self, others, and world until nothing is wrong enough to hurt (per Naranjo, same source). What it defends against is being *trapped* — held still in suffering, boredom, or limitation with no exit and no reframe (per Daniels/Palmer lineage, drdaviddaniels.com above). The fraudulence is structural, not moral: the map of anticipated experience quietly replaces the territory of lived experience, and the Seven sincerely believes the map.

## 4. Recognition Keys

Behavioral / experiential signatures in narrative material — beats, scenes, choices:

1. **The exit is scouted before the room is entered.** A character who accepts every invitation while privately keeping a back door open — the second offer, the return ticket, the parallel plan — and visibly relaxes only when the escape route is confirmed.
2. **Anticipation outshines arrival.** The planning scene glows (maps, lists, pitches, itineraries, wooing) while the achievement scene deflates or is skipped entirely; the character is already narrating the next venture at the celebration of this one.
3. **Reframing in real time.** Bad news lands and, within a beat, is retold as good news — the firing becomes a liberation, the breakup an adventure — and the character sincerely believes the retelling; grief is skipped, not suppressed with visible effort.
4. **The good reason.** Self-interest arrives dressed in altruism, and the character's explanations are so fluent, so plausible, that others (and the character) only later notice the appetite underneath the reasoning.
5. **Charm deployed as pressure escapes.** When a scene turns heavy — confrontation, commitment, someone else's pain — the character gets funnier, more dazzling, more talkative; the humor is the sound of a door being felt for.
6. **Commitment approached and abandoned at the threshold.** Enthusiastic beginnings, cold-blooded exits: projects, apprenticeships, loves seized "with extraordinary enthusiasm only to abandon them" once they harden into limitation (imagery per the future-oriented intuitive Naranjo cites approvingly, ch. 5).
7. **Sideways rebellion.** Never a frontal fight with authority: the character agrees, charms, and then simply does what they wanted — "I did not confront him, but I did what I felt, in spite of his rules" (verbatim first-person account quoted by Naranjo, ch. 5, claudionaranjo.net PDF).
8. **Tools accumulated instead of work done.** Courses, gear, books, contacts, credentials — a hoard of *capacity* for lives never quite begun (per Naranjo on sublimation: cathexis of one's own resourcefulness, "they tend to accumulate tools for doing rather than simply doing," verbatim).
9. **The equalizing move.** The character instinctively flattens hierarchy — first names with the king, jokes with the judge — because taking authority seriously would mean taking limits seriously.
10. **The burst balloon.** When escape genuinely fails — cornered, bored, confined — the buoyancy snaps into edgy irritability or sudden desolation, an emptiness that seems to come from nowhere because it was there all along.

## 5. False Positives

Adjacent territory commonly mistaken for the Seven's struggle:

1. **Appetite as domination (Type 8 territory).** Intensity-seeking through confrontation, excess as an assertion of power, pleasure taken *by force of will* in the concrete here-and-now. Naranjo explicitly contrasts the two: the Eight's rebellion is open and direct; the Seven's is "indirect and subtle," an anti-conventionalism that talks its way around obstacles rather than smashing through them. If the character wants to *win* the pleasure, it's lust, not gluttony.
2. **Optimism in service of image (Type 3 territory).** Relentless positivity performed to be *seen as* successful, calibrated to an audience's applause. The narrative tradition notes Sevens are "less concerned with image and other people's approval than other types" (verbatim, TNE Nine Types Guide PDF); the Seven's cheer is for their own inner weather, not the market.
3. **Contingency planning from dread (Type 6 territory).** Planning that maps dangers, worst cases, and who can be trusted is the Six's vigilance. The Seven's planning maps *pleasures* — it is appetitive, not protective; it multiplies delights rather than rehearsing threats.
4. **Pleasing to be needed (Type 2 territory).** Warmth and generosity aimed at securing a particular person's dependence and love. The Seven's seductive amiability is diffuse and equalitarian — a general brightening of the room in service of keeping experience pleasant, not a targeted courtship of indispensability.
5. **Comfort as self-forgetting (Type 9 territory).** Easygoing pleasantness that comes from erasing one's own agenda and merging with others' plans. The Seven never loses the agenda; the appetite stays vividly *theirs* — options multiply around their own desire, not around keeping the peace.
