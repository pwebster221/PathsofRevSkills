## The function stack (order)

The INTJ uses, in order:
1. Introverted Intuition (Ni) — dominant
2. Extraverted Thinking (Te) — auxiliary
3. Introverted Feeling (Fi) — tertiary
4. Extraverted Sensing (Se) — inferior

(Confirmed across all five sources; Priebe renders the stack as "Ni – Te – Fi – Se".)

## Heidi Priebe — the one-line "manifest" of each function in its INTJ position

> Dominant Introverted Intuition (Ni): "Everything is interconnected and I must
> determine the meaning and implication behind those connections."

> Auxiliary Extroverted Thinking (Te): "Now that I have determined the best or most
> reliable course of action, I will set it into motion using the most straightforward
> method available to me."

> Tertiary Introverted Feeling (Fi): "I must adhere to my values and morals at all costs
> – even if doing so is unpleasant."

> Inferior Extroverted Sensing (Se): "I must analyze all possible outcomes of a sensory
> experience, as the physical world is subject to change unexpectedly, at any time."

(Source: Priebe, "How Each Cognitive Function Manifests Based On Its Position In Your
Stacking.")

## Heidi Priebe — recognizing the Ni-dominant in real life

## Susan Storm — the INTJ cognitive function stack (felt texture)

Dominant Introverted Intuition (Ni):

> "Introverted Intuition (Ni) gives INTJs the ability to future-prospect and witness
> present-day reality from a broad perspective."

> "Ni insights well up from the unconscious at its own pace, cluing the INTJ into the
> thematic narratives playing out in their lives."

> "Ni is often equated to seemingly out-of-nowhere 'Aha!' moments."

> "INTJs come to trust and rely on their deeper mind's ability to organize their
> perceptions in the background."

Auxiliary Extraverted Thinking (Te):

> "Auxiliary Te simply tells the INTJ how they can best make that happen."

> "Te prioritizes 'what works' in reality."

> "Motivated by Te, INTJs are acutely aware that time is a resource that cannot be
> replenished."

> "When utilized together, well-developed NiTe skillfully actualizes dreams."

Tertiary Introverted Feeling (Fi):

> "Fi ultimately serves the INTJ's Dominant Introverted Intuition (Ni) through directing
> goals and ambitions towards achievements with personal significance."

> "For the INTJ, deep sentiment comes from an inner place."

> "Emotional attachment is not an external show for the other person so much as place to
> hold an internal sense of moral rectitude and integrity."

Inferior Extraverted Sensing (Se):

> "INTJs can be so focused on the ultimate outcome of a particular endeavor that they
> never partake in the pleasure of now."

> "INTJs may focus so heavily on the thematic structuring of their experience that they
> never actually *live*."

(Source: Storm, "The INTJ Cognitive Function Stack.")

## A.J. Drenth — Ni, Te, Fi, and inferior Se

> "Intuitive insight often seems to emerge ex nihilo, as a welcomed but unexpected gift."

> "Te is ultimately more of a servant than a master. It is primarily used to analyze or
> flesh out Ni's intuitions."

> "Fi is associated with intensive emotional investments in a limited number of
> individuals."

> "Having Se as their inferior function makes INTJs less attuned to the details or
> concrete elements of life."

> "Many INTJs report feeling like aliens or strangers in the world, even estranged from
> their own bodies."

(Source: Drenth, "INTJ Personality Type Profile.")

## Susan Storm — signs of the UNHEALTHY INTJ (the shadow pole)

> "Unhealthy INTJs get tunnel vision about their ideas and visions."

> "Unhealthy INTJs legitimately don't care how other people feel." (Storm italicizes "care".)

> "Unhealthy INTJs will bend the facts and the logic to fit their vision or idea."

(Source: Storm, "10 Signs of an Unhealthy INTJ." Storm also describes the grip of
inferior Se — sensory/dopamine-seeking overdrive, impulsivity, obsessive cleaning or
exercise — and Ni over-inflation into closed-mindedness; these are SUMMARIZED in the
SKILL, not quoted, because the fetched wording could not be confirmed verbatim.)
