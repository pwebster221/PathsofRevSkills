## 1. Extraverted Sensing (Se) — Drenth, "Se vs. Si"

Source: A.J. Drenth, "Extraverted Sensing (Se) vs. Introverted Sensing (Si),"
*Personality Junkie*.
URL: https://personalityjunkie.com/extraverted-sensing-se-vs-introverted-sensing-si/

> "Extraverted Sensing (or what Jung called Extraverted Sensation) occurs by way of the
> five primary senses (sight, sound, touch, smell, and taste)."

> "Se can be understood to involve a concern for the concrete events happening around us."

> "SPs seek pleasure and Se takes great pleasure in perceiving both physical beauty and
> sensory novelty."

> "Their penchant for sensory novelty is why SPs are commonly described as thrill-seekers
> or hedonists."

Direct Se/Si contrast:

> "The introverted functions can generally be considered more conservative and the
> extraverted functions more liberal or expansive. This holds true for Si and Se."

## 2. Introverted Sensing (Si) — Drenth, "Se vs. Si" (same article)

Source: A.J. Drenth, "Extraverted Sensing (Se) vs. Introverted Sensing (Si),"
*Personality Junkie*.
URL: https://personalityjunkie.com/extraverted-sensing-se-vs-introverted-sensing-si/

> "Introverted Sensing (Si), by contrast, relates to inner bodily sensations such as
> pain, hunger, thirst, internal temperature, numbness, tingling, muscle tension, etc."

> "I like to think of Si as a synopsis of one's personal past, in which all of one's past
> experiences are condensed into a specific view of the past."

> "For Si types (SJs), in particular, the things that are most prominent and cherished in
> this Si perspective are those which are most routine and familiar."

## 4. Introverted Sensing (Si) — Drenth, dedicated Si page

Source: A.J. Drenth, "Introverted Sensing / Sensation (Si)," *Personality Junkie*.
URL: https://personalityjunkie.com/09/introverted-sensing-sensation-si/

> "Si provides us access to our life history, as well as other sorts of acquired
> information, which hopefully prevents us from making the same mistakes twice."

> "Si more or less preserves and relays information in its original form."

> "Si provides access to our most basic sense of 'being,' apart from thought or outward
> stimuli."

> "In SJ types, Si often translates into an adherence to existing facts, traditions,
> worldviews, or methods."

## 5. Se and Si — Storm, "What Type of Sensor Are You?"

Source: Susan Storm, "How Different Personality Types Use Sensation (Se or Si)" / "What
Type of Sensor Are You? The Difference Between Extraverted and Introverted Sensing,"
*Psychology Junkie*.
URL: https://www.psychologyjunkie.com/what-type-of-sensor-are-you-the-difference-between-extraverted-and-introverted-sensing/

On Extraverted Sensing (Se):

> "Extraverted sensation is focused on taking in the details and sensations of the world
> around you."

> "They notice sensory details and sensations like no other type. They are very aware of
> what's going on around them, and can react quickly and spontaneously."

On Introverted Sensing (Si):

> "Introverted sensation types have an awareness and reverie of past experiences. They
> learn from a plethora of personal experiences and readily recall facts and
> tried-and-true techniques that have worked for them before."

> "A flower isn't just a flower, it's a reminder of their grandmother's garden, the smell
> of hand cream, the feeling of soil under their fingertips."

## 6. Function-by-stack-position — Priebe

Source: Heidi Priebe, "How Each Cognitive Function Manifests Based On Its Position In
Your Stacking," *Thought Catalog*, December 2015.
URL: https://thoughtcatalog.com/heidi-priebe/2015/12/how-each-cognitive-function-manifests-based-on-its-position-in-your-stacking/

Extraverted Sensing (Se) as a dominant function:

> "As a dominant function, Se manifests as the desire to engage fully with the sensory
> aspects of one's environment, without any restraint or pause for analysis."

Dominant-Se monologue:

> "I want to sample all of the experiences that are immediately available to me and see
> where those experiences lead me!"

Introverted Sensing (Si) as a dominant function:

> "As a dominant function, Si manifests as a powerful memory for what has worked well in
> the past, and the desire to structure one's life around the traditions and positive
> outcomes of past experiences."
